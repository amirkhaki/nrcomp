# E7: Human Perceptual Validation Study Protocol

**Document Version:** 1.0 (Draft)  
**Project:** `nrcomp` — No-Reference Compression Degradation Predictor from Codec Latents via Proxy Labels  
**Standard Compliance:** ITU-R BT.500-14, ITU-T P.910, JPEG AIC-3 (ISO/IEC 29170-2)  

---

## 1. Executive Summary & Research Objectives

### 1.1 Purpose
The `nrcomp` latent student is trained on proxy labels generated by a full-reference surrogate metric (HaarPSI). However, existing public human-annotated compression datasets (e.g., MCL-JCI, KonJND-1k, JPEG AI SDR25) evaluate legacy transform codecs (JPEG, JPEG 2000, HEVC) or different neural codecs.

This protocol specifies a controlled psychophysical experiment to collect ground-truth human subjective quality judgments on images compressed specifically with the **ELiC neural codec** across all 6 operating points ($\lambda \in \{0.0016, 0.0036, 0.0075, 0.015, 0.03, 0.06\}$).

### 1.2 Primary Hypotheses
1. **$H_1$ (Human Agreement):** The latent student's degradation predictions ($s$) correlate as strongly with human perceptual rankings (measured in Just Objectionable Differences, JOD) as the full-reference proxy teacher ($y = \text{HaarPSI}$).
2. **$H_2$ (Intra-Rate Discrimination):** At a fixed compression rate $\lambda$, the student's rankings reflect human perception better than bitrate shortcuts ($bpp\_actual$) or standard general-purpose no-reference models (e.g., CLIPIQA, NIQE).
3. **$H_3$ (Teacher Resolution):** Human observers will resolve the divergence between HaarPSI and DISTS regarding neural texture synthesis artifacts.

## 2. Stimuli Selection & Design

### 2.1 Source Images
- **Total Source Images:** $N_{\text{src}} = 40$ natural images.
  - 30 images sampled from **KonIQ-10k** ($1024 \times 768$).
  - 10 images sampled from **LiveC** ($500 \times 500$, evaluating cross-resolution behavior).
- **Stratification:** Stratified uniformly across 3 source quality tertiles based on original MOS (`orig_mos`):
  - 1/3 Low-quality source images (slight motion blur, camera sensor noise).
  - 1/3 Medium-quality source images.
  - 1/3 Pristine / High-quality source images.
- **Content Diversity:** Covers portraits/faces, architectural structures, high-frequency natural textures (foliage, water), and smooth gradient regions (sky).

### 2.2 Compression Operating Points
Each source image is encoded and decoded with the pinned ELiC models across all 6 lambdas:
$$\lambda_0 = 0.0016, \quad \lambda_1 = 0.0036, \quad \lambda_2 = 0.0075, \quad \lambda_3 = 0.015, \quad \lambda_4 = 0.03, \quad \lambda_5 = 0.06$$
Total compressed stimuli: $40 \text{ sources} \times 6 \text{ lambdas} = 240 \text{ stimuli}$ (+ 40 uncompressed references = 280 images).

## 3. Psychophysical Methodology

### 3.1 Test Paradigm: Two-Alternative Forced Choice (2AFC)
To maximize discriminatory sensitivity for subtle intra-rate differences, we employ a **Double Stimulus Forced Choice (2AFC)** pairwise comparison protocol, augmented with reference toggle:
1. **Intra-Rate Comparison:** Pairs of different images compressed at the **same** operating point $\lambda$ are evaluated for relative degradation.
2. **Inter-Rate Verification:** Adjacent-rate pairs $(\lambda_k, \lambda_{k+1})$ of the same image are evaluated to confirm monotonicity anchors.
3. **Presentation:** Side-by-side presentation on a calibrated display with synchronized zoom and pan. Observers can press a toggle key (Spacebar) to momentarily view the pristine uncompressed original for both images.
4. **Task Prompt:** *"Which compressed image preserves the quality of its original better?"* (Forced choice: Left or Right).

### 3.2 Display & Viewing Conditions (ITU-R BT.500)
- **Display Device:** 27-inch IPS LCD, calibrated to sRGB gamut, D65 color temperature, gamma 2.2.
- **Peak Luminance:** $150 \pm 10 \text{ cd/m}^2$.
- **Room Illumination:** Controlled dim lighting ($< 20 \text{ lux}$).
- **Viewing Distance:** Fixed at $3.2 \times$ picture height ($H$), maintained via a comfortable chin-rest.

### 3.3 Observers & Screening
- **Observer Count:** $N_{\text{obs}} \ge 25$ naive subjects (non-experts in image compression).
- **Vision Pre-Screening:**
  - Snellen chart test (corrected visual acuity $\ge 20/20$).
  - Ishihara 24-plate test (normal color vision).
- **Training Phase:** 10 trial pairs (not included in the evaluation set) illustrating severe blur, color shift, and near-lossless compression.

## 4. Psychometric Scaling & Data Analysis

### 4.1 Probabilistic Choice Modeling (Bradley-Terry-Luce)
From the binary choice matrices $C_{ij}$ (number of times stimulus $i$ was preferred over $j$), psychometric quality scale values $\mu_i$ in **Just Objectionable Differences (JOD)** are estimated using maximum likelihood under the Bradley-Terry model:
$$P(i \succ j) = \frac{1}{1 + 10^{(\mu_j - \mu_i) / \sigma}}$$
where 1 JOD unit represents a 75% preference probability in a forced-choice comparison.

### 4.2 Observer Screening & Outlier Removal
- Subject reliability is evaluated using **Kendall's coefficient of consistency** ($\zeta$) on circular triads ($A \succ B \succ C \succ A$).
- Observers whose intra-subject consistency falls below the 95% confidence threshold ($\zeta < 0.65$) are discarded and replaced.

### 4.3 Validation Metrics
The resulting subjective ground-truth scores ($\text{JOD}$) will be evaluated against:
1. **$\hat{s}_{\text{latent}}$ (Latent Student Conditioned B)**
2. **$y$ (HaarPSI Proxy Label)**
3. **$1 - \text{DISTS}$ (Deep Feature Proxy Label)**
4. **$bpp\_actual$ (Bitrate Shortcut Baseline)**

Metrics evaluated:
- Overall SROCC, PLCC, and RMSE (with 95% bootstrap CIs).
- **Within-$\lambda$ SROCC** (assessing whether the student predicts true human-perceived distortion differences between images at the same rate).
- Linear Mixed-Effects Model (LMM) testing the interaction between metric predictions and source MOS tertiles.

## 6. Significance for the Field
Executing this study will establish the **first publicly available human benchmark dataset specifically targeting learned neural codec artifacts (ELiC)**, validating whether latent-space features can permanently replace pixel-domain decoding for quality assessment in cloud streaming and edge compression.
